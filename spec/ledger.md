# Working ledger: points to revisit while writing UNAUTHZ.md

> Status, 2026-11-06. Everything below is under revalidation after a reset. The next commit of this file keeps only what was validated and is prepared to be defined in the model; the rest is removed. Cite nothing here as decided.

Transitional file beside the draft. Each entry names the section it belongs to, the point, and what resolving it looks like. Entries leave this file when the section absorbs them.

Evidence base. Sentences below marked "Evidence" come from a study made on 2026-11-06 and 2026-11-07 of eleven platforms, each separated feature by feature into what its decision component computes, what its store keeps and what the product around them does, from the platform's own specification, documentation and, where public, source code: Kubernetes RBAC, WordPress roles and capabilities, GitHub, GitLab, Google Drive, Azure RBAC with Microsoft Entra ID, Google Cloud IAM, AWS IAM with Organizations, Discord, Notion, and the AT Protocol with Bluesky. Counts such as "9 of 11" count platforms out of these eleven. These sentences stand on their own; nothing in the draft cites or depends on them.

## Order of work

1. Who belongs to what and what is acted on, as relations: written into the draft (subject and groups, Section 7; places and coverage, Section 6).
2. How permissions and place keys map to each other in storage and in carriers: written into the draft (entries keyed by place, bases, the three outcomes, canonical form, Section 8; what a store must and should answer, Section 9).

- Grants carry no expiry: a statement that expires after grants were issued stays in the carrier. Either an entry carries the earliest expiry of what it holds, or grants live no longer than the soonest expiry among the statements they came from. Evidence: seven of the eleven platforms let a grant expire; five of them stop counting it at evaluation time, one stores the date and purges the grant with a nightly job, one keeps a schedule that must be activated.

## Section 7, Decision

- **A question with several permissions.** A question names one permission today. A call site sometimes needs several at once (editing someone else's published post), or any one of several (viewing a page that either of two permissions opens). To define in the model, before the closing paragraph below is taken up: a question MAY name several permissions and says whether all of them or any of them must be satisfied, each permission decided as a single question is, the answer carrying one reason per permission or the one that settled it. Open: whether the reason of an "all" question that fails names every failing permission or the first one, and whether grants reads carry the same shape. Evidence: six of the eleven platforms have checks that need several permissions at once, five meet it in the application and one in the engine, where every permission the mapping returns must be held.

## Section 8, Grants

- **Non-redundant grants: what is written in the draft and what remains.** Written: a grant holds only what its own place gives, allows and denies; reads walk the chain; wildcards carried as written; presets carried by name; stored roles carried as their resolved permissions. Remaining: the format itself (field names, how allows, denies and preset names sit in one entry). Decided by the author for the version string: it is made of components, one per thing whose change makes carried grants worth recomputing, namely the catalog with its presets, the structure, the engine, and the minifier when one is used; each component changes only when its own input changes in a way that matters, so an application comparing the string learns that a recompute is due, wherever it keeps its sessions. Still to define: what exactly each component fingerprints, and which engine changes count.
- **Stored roles go stale in a carrier, and that is accepted.** A stored role is copied into grants as its resolved permissions, so an edit to the role is invisible to a carrier already issued; nothing in the carrier can reveal it. This is the known limit of any self-contained token and is accepted, as it is for tokens in general. What Section 8 should still say: the exposure lasts until the grants are next issued, so the lifetime of grants bounds it; an application that wants it shorter keeps a counter on its side, per subject or per stored role, stamps it into grants and compares it when it refreshes a session, which reads the application's own session data and never the statements. To verify against their texts before citing: the recommendation of short-lived tokens in the OAuth security best current practice, token revocation and introspection (RFC 7009, RFC 7662), and the OpenID Shared Signals and Continuous Access Evaluation work, which exists because of this limit.
- **Wildcards carried as written is on trial.** Chosen without evidence either way. Revisit when an implementation can measure carrier size and read cost for both forms, and confirm that a wildcard read against the catalog cannot answer differently from the statements it came from.
- **Prior art still to verify before citing in the draft.** Carrying compact facts and deriving at read: RFC 9068 (`groups`, `roles`, `entitlements` claims) and Kerberos group identifiers were named from memory; Biscuit tokens and RFC 9396 authorization details were not checked against their texts. Evidence from the eleven platforms on carrying computed access: one keeps an effective level per user and project, computed by a query and refreshed by background workers, accepting staleness; one fixes a token's permissions for the token's life and re-resolves them on refresh; one carries group membership in the token and caches assignments for up to thirty minutes; one re-evaluates every request against current policy and carries nothing.

- **A trie layout for `g` (idea).** Keying entries by place repeats common key prefixes. A nested layout, one object per segment, measured 60 percent smaller uncompressed and 5 percent smaller after gzip on a sample of 105 places. To be tested against the flat one-entry-per-key reads before any proposal, since a store that keeps one row per key and a reader that walks the chain both assume the flat form.
- **Minified form of grants.** The model defines one canonical form of grants: place keys and permissions written in full. An application MAY carry grants in a **minified** form: a substitution that replaces each word of the model with a shorter token, the way Morse code gives the most frequent letters the shortest signals. What Section 8 must define:
  - The substitution works word by word, never on the payload as a whole. The words are the level names of the structure, the resources and actions of the catalog, and the names of presets. Values of segments (`org-1`, `u7`) are application data the model does not know, so they stay as they are; the application may shorten its own ids on its side, outside the model.
  - The dictionary is derived deterministically from the catalog, the structure and the minifier version, and from nothing else, so it never travels with the grants and two parties with the same three inputs build the same dictionary. Frequency comes from the catalog itself: an action that appears under many resources (`read`, `manage`) earns a one-character token, a resource or level that appears once earns a longer one.
  - The substitution is injective: distinct words get distinct tokens, and a token is never a prefix of another in a position where it could be confused, so the minified form reads back without ambiguity. The lossless round-trip is the Section 4 invariant.
  - The version string of grants identifies the minifier and its version next to the catalog and structure fingerprints; a change in any of the three forces a reissue. Without a minifier the version carries no minifier part.
  - Because the substitution is word by word, a reader answers a question by minifying the chain keys of the target and the asked permission and matching tokens against the carried entries, with the same result as expanding every entry first. An engine still reads and writes canonical forms only; the minifier is a tool at the carrier boundary, library-provided or application-provided, never part of the engine.

## Seen on several platforms, not in the model

To expand later, after the entries above are drained. Four constructs recur across the eleven platforms and have no counterpart in the draft: a stored deny about a subject that the platform's customers write and that beats any grant (two platforms; close to the model's personal deny); a condition attached to a grant and evaluated with attributes of the thing, the request or the time (six, the four infrastructure platforms among them); a narrower place holding less than the wider one (three, all content or chat products, by a flag on the folder, an overwrite, or a restricted subpage); a boundary attached to the principal rather than the place, capping what it may reach anywhere (two). None is copied; each is a question of whether an invariant of this model needs it or whether the guidance shows how an application gets it.
