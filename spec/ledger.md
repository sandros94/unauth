# Working ledger: points to revisit while writing UNAUTHZ.md

Transitional file beside the draft. Each entry names the section it belongs to, the point, and what resolving it looks like. Entries leave this file when the section absorbs them.

## Section 6, Place

- **Appendix C grammar for keys.** Write the ABNF: `place = "/" / 1*segment`, `segment = "/" level "/" value`, level lowercase ASCII identifier, value `1*pchar` per RFC 3986 with the canonical percent-encoding rule, reserved values `*`, `.`, `..`, no trailing `/`, no empty segment. Keys must be valid RFC 3986 `path-absolute`.
- **Singleton levels.** With one unnamed root, a top that has exactly one instance (the platform side next to tenants) still needs a value, `/platform/main`. Decide whether the structure may declare a level as a singleton, printed with a fixed value so that keys stay parseable without the structure, or whether the dummy value is accepted as is.
- **Open structure and maximum depth.** Decide whether an application may declare an open structure (any level name, any nesting, grammar checking only) for prototypes, and how a maximum depth is declared for self-nesting levels. Both are declarations; the engine never assumes either.

## Section 7, Decision

- **Which statement is reported as the reason.** When several statements decide the same way (two allows, or two denies, covering the target), the decision names one of them. The old draft said "the first in input order", which depends on the store's ordering and breaks determinism across stores. Define an order that depends only on the statements: for example the nearest place first, then rule before assignment, then a stable order on the permission and role name.
- **Subject-less, place-wide statements.** Every statement is about one subject. "Freeze unit u7, nobody publishes" or "this tenant is suspended" has no expression other than one deny per subject, which a platform with millions of subjects cannot afford in writes or in time. Study whether the model should carry statements with no subject that apply to everyone at a place and under it: how they combine with the per-subject ceiling (a place-wide deny vetoes like any deny; a place-wide allow would be a default grant to everyone there), who may place them under the authority rules, how grants carry them (they are not per subject, so a grants carrier would need a place-wide part or the engine would need to consult them separately), and whether the recovery theorem still holds with them in play.
- **Guest sharing under a wider deny.** A document shared with an outside guest is an allow at `/org/org-1/doc/d42`. If the tenant also carries a deny of `doc:read` on that guest at `/org/org-1`, the deny wins: a deny is a ban and a share does not undo it. "Guests cannot read by default" is default-deny, not a deny rule, so a share alone works. Nearest-first precedence applies to nothing in this model: allows accumulate along the chain, denies veto everywhere. The worked example must show both cases.
- **Deny of the wildcard `*`.** Hypothesis: a rule denying `*` is legal and acts as a gate or ban at the place where it is placed, what it gates being application logic (a suspended account, a frozen tenant). Since deny is exact, a deny of `*` denies each permission exactly, so it beats every allow at any covering place, including an allow of `*`. Revalidate while writing the decision procedure: interaction with "deny is exact, never implied", with implications, with grants issuance (an empty entry or an explicit marker), and with authority (who may place a deny of `*`, given "hand out only what you hold" and "restrictions come from at least as wide a place").
- **Wildcards and implications.** A role `*:*` minus `billing:read` still yields `billing:manage`, and `billing:manage` implies `billing:read`, so the holder reads billing anyway. Consistent with "deny is exact, implications never deny" and with the author's choice of `*:*`, but the decision procedure must state it and the worked example should show it once.

## Section 8, Grants

- **Minified form of grants.** The model defines one canonical form of grants: place keys and permissions written in full. An application MAY carry grants in a **minified** form: a substitution that replaces each word of the model with a shorter token, the way Morse code gives the most frequent letters the shortest signals. What Section 8 must define:
  - The substitution works word by word, never on the payload as a whole. The words are the level names of the structure, and the resources and actions of the catalog. Values of segments (`org-1`, `u7`) are application data the model does not know, so they stay as they are; the application may shorten its own ids on its side, outside the model.
  - The dictionary is derived deterministically from the catalog, the structure and the minifier version, and from nothing else, so it never travels with the grants and two parties with the same three inputs build the same dictionary. Frequency comes from the catalog itself: an action that appears under many resources (`read`, `manage`) earns a one-character token, a resource or level that appears once earns a longer one.
  - The substitution is injective: distinct words get distinct tokens, and a token is never a prefix of another in a position where it could be confused, so the minified form reads back without ambiguity. The lossless round-trip is the Section 4 invariant.
  - The version string of grants identifies the minifier and its version next to the catalog and structure fingerprints; a change in any of the three forces a reissue. Without a minifier the version carries no minifier part.
  - Because the substitution is word by word, a reader answers a question by minifying the chain keys of the target and the asked permission and matching tokens against the carried entries, with the same result as expanding every entry first. An engine still reads and writes canonical forms only; the minifier is a tool at the carrier boundary, library-provided or application-provided, never part of the engine.

## Section 10, Authority

- **Editing a referenced role.** Adding a permission to `support` adds it to every role that references `support`, at every place where any of those roles is assigned. Decide who may edit an upstream role and at which place they must hold what they add. This is the escalation surface the old audit hit (a stored role that reached the root) and merges with the open question on stored-role ownership and where a stored role may be assigned.
- **Deleting or renaming a referenced role.** Refuse while references exist, or cascade; decide, and decide what the audit record shows.
- **Role name spaces.** A stored role MUST NOT reuse a preset's name: references are by name, so a duplicate could redirect a reference. State where uniqueness is enforced (definition, validation, both).
