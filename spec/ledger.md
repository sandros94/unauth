# Working ledger: points to revisit while writing UNAUTHZ.md

> Status, 2026-11-06. Everything below is under revalidation after a reset. The next commit of this file keeps only what was validated and is prepared to be defined in the model; the rest is removed. Cite nothing here as decided.

Transitional file beside the draft. Each entry names the section it belongs to, the point, and what resolving it looks like. Entries leave this file when the section absorbs them.

Evidence base. Sentences below marked "Evidence" come from a study made on 2026-11-06 and 2026-11-07 of eleven platforms, each separated feature by feature into what its decision component computes, what its store keeps and what the product around them does, from the platform's own specification, documentation and, where public, source code: Kubernetes RBAC, WordPress roles and capabilities, GitHub, GitLab, Google Drive, Azure RBAC with Microsoft Entra ID, Google Cloud IAM, AWS IAM with Organizations, Discord, Notion, and the AT Protocol with Bluesky. Counts such as "9 of 11" count platforms out of these eleven. These sentences stand on their own; nothing in the draft cites or depends on them.

## Order of work

1. Who belongs to what and what is acted on, as relations: written into the draft (subject and sets, Section 7; places and coverage, Section 6).
2. How permissions and place keys map to each other in storage and in carriers: written for carriers (entries keyed by place, bases, the three outcomes, canonical form, Section 8); for the statements store, Section 9 still has to say that a read for issuing takes a subject and its sets and returns their statements and the stored roles those name, and that selecting everything under a place is a byte range from the key followed by `/` to the key followed by `0`, never a pattern.

- Grants carry no expiry: a statement that expires after grants were issued stays in the carrier. Either an entry carries the earliest expiry of what it holds, or grants live no longer than the soonest expiry among the statements they came from. Evidence: seven of the eleven platforms let a grant expire; five of them stop counting it at evaluation time, one stores the date and purges the grant with a nightly job, one keeps a schedule that must be activated.
- A call site sometimes needs all of several permissions at once (editing someone else's published post). The application combines them today; a helper is wanted. Evidence: six of the eleven platforms have checks that need several permissions at once; five meet it in the application and one in the engine, where every permission the mapping returns must be held.
- A stored role that references a preset costs a document store a wasted read, since a store cannot know which names are presets. The contract for loading stored roles may need the names to skip.

## Section 6, Place

- **A place says where, but nothing says what a place is about.** A level is a name and a position in the structure; the model does not relate it to the catalog. Nothing states which resources live at or under a level, so nothing says that `billing:read` is meaningless at a document, or that a statement at an organisation is about the repositories inside it. Existing systems all tie the two: Kubernetes resources are namespaced or cluster-scoped, Google Cloud permissions belong to resource types that sit at fixed levels of the hierarchy, Zanzibar relations are declared per object type. Study what the structure should declare about each level (the resources it contains, the permissions that are meaningful there, whether the level is itself a resource) before more of Section 6 is settled. This is an open problem, not a wording issue: membership, public levels and singleton levels all depend on its answer. Evidence: on none of the eleven platforms studied does the engine validate a place against a declared structure or know which resources live at a level; it receives a chain, an ancestry or a parent id from the application. The question is therefore whether the model needs a structure at all beyond the key grammar, or only the chain.

- **Appendix C grammar for keys.** Write the ABNF: `place = "/" / 1*segment`, `segment = "/" level "/" value`, level lowercase ASCII identifier, value `1*pchar` per RFC 3986 with the canonical percent-encoding rule, reserved values `*`, `.`, `..`, no trailing `/`, no empty segment. Keys must be valid RFC 3986 `path-absolute`.
- **Singleton levels, open structure and maximum depth.** With one unnamed root, a top that has exactly one instance still needs a value; whether an application may declare an open structure and whether depth is bounded are the same question seen from other sides. Evidence: no platform's engine knows these; they are application decisions unless an invariant needs them.

## Section 5, Catalog

- **Open access is the application's.** A reserved subject for "anyone", a list of levels where it could be placed and a permission gating it were drafted and withdrawn on 2026-11-06. What is open to anyone is defined by the application in its own terms, without an engine or a session; where it wants coverage by place, it uses an ordinary subject of its own. The model reserves no subject and no permission name.

- **Structured resource names (idea, not proposed).** An application names the state of a thing on the resource (`private-post:edit`), and the model treats `post` and `private-post` as unrelated. Nothing then says "this action on every state of this one resource" in one entry, and a wildcard on the action reaches every state. Kubernetes writes a variant as `pods/log` under the same verbs. Allowing such a form would change what a permission is, so it needs a need that naming and exclusions cannot meet. Evidence for naming the state on the resource: every standard and engine surveyed ([XACML 3.0](https://docs.oasis-open.org/xacml/3.0/xacml-3.0-core-spec-os-en.html), NIST SP 800-162, [Cedar](https://docs.cedarpolicy.com/policies/policy-examples.html), SpiceDB, Open Policy Agent) carries it on the resource; the action carries it only on four cloud platforms, each inconsistent with itself, and mostly for operations that exist only in that state.

## Section 8, Grants

- **Non-redundant grants: what is written in the draft and what remains.** Written: a grant holds only what its own place gives, allows and denies; reads walk the chain; wildcards carried as written; presets carried by name; stored roles carried as their resolved permissions. Remaining: the format itself (field names, how allows, denies and preset names sit in one entry). Decided by the author for the version string: it is made of components, one per thing whose change makes carried grants worth recomputing, namely the catalog, the structure, the engine together with its presets, and the minifier when one is used; each component changes only when its own input changes in a way that matters, so an application comparing the string learns that a recompute is due, wherever it keeps its sessions. Still to define: what exactly each component fingerprints, and which engine changes count.
- **Stored roles go stale in a carrier, and that is accepted.** A stored role is copied into grants as its resolved permissions, so an edit to the role is invisible to a carrier already issued; nothing in the carrier can reveal it. This is the known limit of any self-contained token and is accepted, as it is for tokens in general. What Section 8 should still say: the exposure lasts until the grants are next issued, so the lifetime of grants bounds it; an application that wants it shorter keeps a counter on its side, per subject or per stored role, stamps it into grants and compares it when it refreshes a session, which reads the application's own session data and never the statements. To verify against their texts before citing: the recommendation of short-lived tokens in the OAuth security best current practice, token revocation and introspection (RFC 7009, RFC 7662), and the OpenID Shared Signals and Continuous Access Evaluation work, which exists because of this limit.
- **Wildcards carried as written is on trial.** Chosen without evidence either way. Revisit when an implementation can measure carrier size and read cost for both forms, and confirm that a wildcard read against the catalog cannot answer differently from the statements it came from.
- **Prior art still to verify before citing in the draft.** Carrying compact facts and deriving at read: RFC 9068 (`groups`, `roles`, `entitlements` claims) and Kerberos group identifiers were named from memory; Biscuit tokens and RFC 9396 authorization details were not checked against their texts. Evidence from the eleven platforms on carrying computed access: one keeps an effective level per user and project, computed by a query and refreshed by background workers, accepting staleness; one fixes a token's permissions for the token's life and re-resolves them on refresh; one carries group membership in the token and caches assignments for up to thirty minutes; one re-evaluates every request against current policy and carries nothing.

- **Freshness with groups.** Grants hold what the subject's and its groups' statements gave at issuing, without recording which gave what; a change in who belongs to a group, or in a statement about a group, is answered only by reissuing. Constraint from the author: answering a question at an endpoint must not cost a round trip for membership. Open: whether the version string should carry a component that the application bumps when membership changes, so that a stale carrier is detected rather than trusted.

- **Minified form of grants.** The model defines one canonical form of grants: place keys and permissions written in full. An application MAY carry grants in a **minified** form: a substitution that replaces each word of the model with a shorter token, the way Morse code gives the most frequent letters the shortest signals. What Section 8 must define:
  - The substitution works word by word, never on the payload as a whole. The words are the level names of the structure, the resources and actions of the catalog, and the names of presets. Values of segments (`org-1`, `u7`) are application data the model does not know, so they stay as they are; the application may shorten its own ids on its side, outside the model.
  - The dictionary is derived deterministically from the catalog, the structure and the minifier version, and from nothing else, so it never travels with the grants and two parties with the same three inputs build the same dictionary. Frequency comes from the catalog itself: an action that appears under many resources (`read`, `manage`) earns a one-character token, a resource or level that appears once earns a longer one.
  - The substitution is injective: distinct words get distinct tokens, and a token is never a prefix of another in a position where it could be confused, so the minified form reads back without ambiguity. The lossless round-trip is the Section 4 invariant.
  - The version string of grants identifies the minifier and its version next to the catalog and structure fingerprints; a change in any of the three forces a reissue. Without a minifier the version carries no minifier part.
  - Because the substitution is word by word, a reader answers a question by minifying the chain keys of the target and the asked permission and matching tokens against the carried entries, with the same result as expanding every entry first. An engine still reads and writes canonical forms only; the minifier is a tool at the carrier boundary, library-provided or application-provided, never part of the engine.

## Section 10, Authority

- **Editing a referenced role.** Adding a permission to `support` adds it to every role that references `support`, at every place where any of those roles is assigned. Decide who may edit an upstream role and at which place they must hold what they add. This is the escalation surface the old audit hit (a stored role that reached the root) and merges with the open question on stored-role ownership and where a stored role may be assigned.
- **Deleting or renaming a referenced role.** Refuse while references exist, or cascade; decide, and decide what the audit record shows.
- **Role name spaces.** A stored role MUST NOT reuse a preset's name: references are by name, so a duplicate could redirect a reference. State where uniqueness is enforced (definition, validation, both).

## Seen on several platforms, not in the model

To expand later, after the entries above are drained. Four constructs recur across the eleven platforms and have no counterpart in the draft: a stored deny about a subject that the platform's customers write and that beats any grant (two platforms; close to the model's personal deny); a condition attached to a grant and evaluated with attributes of the thing, the request or the time (six, the four infrastructure platforms among them); a narrower place holding less than the wider one (three, all content or chat products, by a flag on the folder, an overwrite, or a restricted subpage); a boundary attached to the principal rather than the place, capping what it may reach anywhere (two). None is copied; each is a question of whether an invariant of this model needs it or whether the guidance shows how an application gets it.
